05 / Whitepaper

Charity without borders.
Help with dignity.

The Charity Box working paper sets out an inclusive mission: when people give what they can, small acts of love can help build a better tomorrow. Version 0.2 describes the intended model; it is not a token sale document, legal opinion, or investment promise.

1. Abstract2. Utility3. Dual-chain design4. Charity model5. Community aid6. Transparency7. Risks

1. Abstract

Charity Box (CBOX) is a proposed dual-chain utility token connecting a browser game ecosystem with a transparent public-benefit mission. It is intended for future game consumption, top-ups, and compatible experiences, while eligible project-controlled fees support charitable organizations and a dedicated community aid fund. Open Charity Box. Open a world of compassion.

2. Utility

The initial utility objective is straightforward: allow future chess, board, card, and casual game experiences to accept CBOX for clearly disclosed in-product uses. The present game hall remains free and uses virtual entertainment points. CBOX functionality will be described as active only after contracts, game integrations, and user protections are deployed.

  • Potential game top-ups and consumption.
  • Potential access to future token-enabled events or features.
  • No claim of profit, yield, price support, or guaranteed exchange value.

3. Dual-chain design

CBOX is planned for Solana and BNB Smart Chain. Before launch, the project should publish the canonical mint and contract addresses, total supply accounting across both networks, bridge or synchronization logic if used, administrative permissions, liquidity policy, and independent security review. Supply must never be counted twice across chains.

4. Charity model

The project intends to transfer eligible project-controlled fees on a defined schedule to recognized international charitable organizations. Its humanitarian purpose is universal: assistance is not limited by nationality, skin color, race, gender, age, or geography. Each report should identify the source period, gross eligible amount, operating deductions if any, net donation, recipient, transaction evidence, and receipt where available.

5. Community aid fund

A separate public-benefit fund is proposed for people facing documented hardship. Anyone who genuinely needs help may apply, whether or not they are a token holder. Applications would be reviewed under published, consistent eligibility and conflict-of-interest rules. Sensitive evidence must remain private, while public reports should be aggregated and anonymized.

6. Transparency and governance

Before funds are accepted, the project should publish responsible entities, wallet controls, approval thresholds, audit arrangements, treasury disclosures, charity recipients, aid criteria, privacy handling, and a process for complaints and appeals. Material policy changes should be announced before they take effect. Founder holdings will be disclosed publicly before participation opens: wallet addresses, allocation share, and lock schedule.

7. Risks and limitations

Digital tokens are volatile and can lose all market value. Smart contracts, bridges, wallets, exchanges, and game integrations may fail or be exploited. Regulatory treatment differs by jurisdiction. Charity status and tax treatment depend on legal structure and recipient. Participation must never be presented as a guaranteed investment or as a substitute for direct charitable giving.
